Setting up a review-request workflow is a 10-minute task. Getting consistent 5-star reviews from it is a playbook. This page covers the playbook: when to ask, what to say, where to send clients, and what to avoid. For the setup walk-through, see [Review requests](/1nspect/getting-started/review-requests).

## When to ask

Timing matters more than wording:

| Timing | Expected response rate |
| --- | --- |
| Immediately after Publish (report still locked) | Low (client hasn't read the report yet) |
| Immediately after Delivery (report unlocked, client just got email) | Medium |
| 1 to 2 days after Delivery | Highest |
| 1 week after Delivery | Diminishing returns |
| 1 month after Delivery | Rare |

**Set your workflow step to fire on the Report Delivered trigger with a 1 to 2 day delay.** This hits the sweet spot: the client has had time to read the report, acted on it (shared with their agent, built a repair request), and the experience is still fresh.

Never use **Report Published** as the trigger if your account locks reports until paid. Published and unpaid means the client cannot read the report; a review request at that moment confuses them.

## What to say in the email

Short, warm, specific. The template that works best:

- **Thank them** by name for choosing you
- **Reference the property** (specific, not generic) by address or by a one-line note (`"your new place on Oak Street"`)
- **Make the ask clear and small**: "If you have 60 seconds, your Google review really helps our small business"
- **One link** (not three). Pick Google or Yelp, not both; the one you pick is the one most clients will use.
- **Reply-to is you**. Make sure the from address shows your company name and the reply-to is your real inbox.

Avoid:

- "Please leave us a 5-star review" (coercive, violates Google's and Yelp's TOS)
- Multiple requests in one email (dilutes the ask)
- Generic property reference (`"your inspection"`)
- Review-gating language ("If you had a great experience, leave a review; if not, email us") which violates FTC rules and most review platform TOS

## Pick Google or Yelp (not both)

**Pick the one platform where you want to build your presence.** Clients will almost always click only one link; splitting the attention halves your conversion.

- **Google** — the right choice for 95%+ of firms. Local-search dominance, higher conversion, better defense against negative reviews.
- **Yelp** — the right choice if your market has a strong Yelp culture (urban coastal, hospitality-adjacent) or you already have a strong Yelp presence to maintain.

Set the chosen link in **Business Ops › Company Info** so the review-link tag renders in your email templates.

## When a client leaves a negative review

- **Respond publicly, calmly, within 24 hours.** No excuses, no counter-attacks. Thank them, acknowledge, offer to make it right offline.
- **Reach out privately** if you can. Many 2 or 3-star reviews get upgraded when the client feels heard.
- **Learn from it.** Was the problem process (we missed something), expectation (they thought we did something we didn't), or personal (bad day)? Adjust accordingly.

## When a client leaves no review

- No follow-up requests. One ask is enough; nagging is counterproductive.
- Don't take it personally. 20 to 30% response rate on review requests is excellent; 10 to 15% is typical.

## Compliance notes

- **Do not offer anything in exchange for a review** (discount on next inspection, gift card, etc.). This violates FTC guidelines and Google's TOS and can get your Google Business Profile suspended.
- **Do not review-gate.** Sending the review link only to clients you expect will leave 5-stars ("If you had a great experience, click here; otherwise email us") violates the FTC's testimonial guidelines.
- **Do not use fake reviews.** The usual temptation. The usual bad outcome.
