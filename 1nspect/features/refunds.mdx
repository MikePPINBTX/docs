Use **Issue Refund** to give some or all of a payment back. If the client paid by card, 1nspecT refunds the card through Stripe. If they paid another way (cash, check, Zelle, Venmo, offline card), 1nspecT records the refund and you return the money yourself. Either way it shows in History, and a **Refund Issued** email can go out.

## Issue a refund

**Issue Refund** appears in two places, next to **Mark as Paid**:

- The order page header.
- A single inspection's **Overview** tab, under **Quick Actions**.

It shows only once the inspection is paid, and disappears once the full amount has been refunded.

Clicking it opens **Issue Refund — inspection number**. The top line tells you which kind of refund this will be:

- *Refunds the client's card through Stripe.* when a card payment is on file.
- *No card payment on file — this records a refund you return yourself (cash, check, Zelle…).* otherwise.

Below that, a summary reads *Paid $X · already refunded $Y · up to $Z can be refunded.*

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Refund amount | Defaults to everything still refundable. Enter the agreed amount; it can be less than what was paid. |
| Reason | Settlement, Cancellation, Overpayment, Service not performed or Other. Shown in History and available to emails as `$RefundReason`. |
| Refunded by | Only for a refund you return yourself: Cash, Check, Zelle, Venmo, Offline Credit Card or Other. |
| Internal note (optional) | History only. Never sent to the client. |

Click **Refund $X** to finish.

### Limits

- **You can refund more than once.** Several partial refunds are fine, up to the amount paid in total. Over that, the amount field shows *More than the $X left to refund.*
- **A double-click refunds once.** Clicking the button twice, or retrying after a slow connection, doesn't refund twice.
- **There's no role restriction.** Anyone on your team who can open the order can issue a refund.

## What the client sees

- **The report stays available.** A refund, even a full one, never locks the report again, and the payment status doesn't change.
- **Card refunds take Stripe's usual time** to reach the client, typically 5 to 10 business days.
- **The invoice isn't regenerated** and doesn't show the refund.
- **A Refund Issued email** goes out for each refund if you have the step switched on (below).

## Refund Issued email

Each refund fires the **Refund Issued** trigger in **Business Ops › Communications › Workflow**. Existing accounts were given a default **Refund Issued** template and step in November 2026 (to the client and co-client, sent immediately). If yours has none, create a template and a step on the **Refund Issued** trigger.

The email can use:

- `$RefundAmount`: the amount of the most recent refund, for example `$150.00`.
- `$RefundReason`: the reason you picked.

The default wording leaves the reason out, since reasons such as "Settlement" read as internal. Add `$RefundReason` if you want it.

## History

Each refund adds a **REFUND ISSUED** row to the inspection's and order's History, under the name of the person who issued it. For example:

- *Refund of $150.00 issued via Stripe (re_…). Reason: Settlement.*
- *Refund of $150.00 recorded (Zelle).*

The internal note appears on the row too.

## Cancel Order and Remove Ancillary

These still refund **card payments automatically**, as before:

| Action | What refunds |
| --- | --- |
| **Cancel Order** (the whole order or one phase) | The paid amount for the cancelled scope |
| **Remove Ancillary** (the X on an ancillary fee line, on a paid order) | That service's share of the paid amount |

They only refund through Stripe. If the order was paid another way, they tell you no refund was made, and they never record one. Use **Issue Refund** to record the money you return.
